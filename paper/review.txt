TITLE: Beyond End-to-End Metrics: Stage-Oriented Evaluation of Cascaded Hybrid Intrusion Detection Systems

----------- Overall evaluation -----------
SCORE: 0 (borderline paper)
----- TEXT:
The paper proposes a stage-oriented way to evaluate cascaded hybrid IDS. Instead of looking only at the final performance of the full system, it measures what the second detector adds. The authors use three measures: Conditional Detection Rate (CDR), Incremental Recall, and Incremental FPR. They test the approach with Snort followed by an Isolation Forest on CICIDS2017.

The question is relevant. Hybrid IDS papers often report only the final Precision, Recall, or F1-score. These values do not clearly show how many attacks are recovered by the second stage or how many new false positives it creates.

The experiments are carefully done. The authors explain how Snort alerts are matched to flows. They reprocess the PCAP files with CICFlowMeter, test different time tolerances, and exclude ambiguous matches. The Isolation Forest is also tested with ten random states. The threshold analysis clearly shows the trade-off between additional detections and false positives.

My first concern is the contribution itself. Incremental Recall and Incremental FPR are differences between standard metrics. CDR is the Recall of the second stage on the attacks missed by the first stage. Reporting these three values together is useful, but the novelty of the paper is limited.

The scope of the framework is also narrow. It applies to cascades where the second stage analyzes traffic that the first stage did not flag. However, other cascade-based IDS architectures exist. For example, some cascades send traffic already marked as suspicious to a second stage for confirmation and false-positive reduction [1]. CDR does not directly apply to this design because the second stage is not recovering attacks missed by the first stage. I would prefer metrics and an evaluation pipeline that generalize to different hybrid, or at least cascade, architectures.

The paper studies a relevant problem, and the experiments are carefully described. However, the novelty of the framework and the scope of its applicability are limited. I consider the paper borderline.


[1] A. Kutlimuratov et al., “A Lightweight Cascade-Based Farmework for Real-Time Zero-Day Attack Detection,” Computers, vol. 15, no. 3, article 174, 2026.

Summary and Strengths

The paper addresses a relevant evaluation problem in cascaded hybrid IDS: end-to-end metrics do not explicitly show the contribution of downstream stages. The proposed use of CDR, \Delta Recall, and \Delta FPR provides an intuitive interpretation of the additional attacks recovered from Snort residuals and the associated false-positive cost.

The experimental protocol is generally careful, including flow-level Snort alert matching, benign-only training and calibration of the Isolation Forest, threshold analysis, multiple random seeds, and class-level reporting.

Main Concerns

The main limitation is the limited novelty and empirical scope. CDR and \Delta Recall are directly related, while the \Delta Recall–\Delta FPR analysis is closely related to a rescaled ROC analysis on residual traffic. The main contribution therefore appears to be interpretability rather than a substantially new evaluation methodology.

The framework is demonstrated using only CICIDS2017 and a single Snort–Isolation Forest cascade. Additional datasets, rule sets, detectors, or cascade configurations would be needed to establish broader applicability. The observed detection gain is also strongly concentrated on DoS/DDoS, while most residual attack classes receive little or no benefit.

Minor Comments

Please clarify that the reported 1% FPR is only the target used for threshold calibration on Monday’s benign validation data, not the FPR observed on the residual evaluation set.

Comparisons between \Delta Recall and \Delta FPR (6.44 pp vs. 1.52 pp) should be phrased carefully, since these metrics use different denominators.
